# Aftersignal — Full Story & Expanded Design

*Working title. This document expands and supersedes Section 2 (Premise/Story) of the original design doc — everything else (art direction, tech plan, folder structure) still applies unless noted.*

---

## Logline

Two salvagers sent to strip a dead research station for parts uncover a twelve-year-old cover-up, a crew that didn't just disappear — they were *replaced*, one by one — and a thing under the ice that has been quietly rehearsing how to be human. By the time they realize the moon isn't the anomaly, it's the *containment*, they have to decide whether to finish what the crew started, undo it, or become the next entry in the log.

---

## Act Structure

Built for roughly a full day of playtime solo-paced (6–8 hours), or a longer, more thorough two-day playthrough for players who read every log and chase every optional puzzle. Structured across the five wings from the original doc, with a new epilogue wing added.

### Act 1 — Arrival (Landing Bay & Habitation Wing)

Players land, get separated by the "shuttle glitch" as originally planned. Tutorial pacing stays the same: movement, radio, basic puzzle-sharing.

**New addition — the first twist seed:** one player finds a **crew roster** with twelve names and photos. The other player, exploring the mess hall, finds **personal effects for only eleven people** — one crew member's quarters are completely bare, like they were never actually assigned a room. Nobody mentions it explicitly; it's just a detail sharp players will clock early and get paid off in Act 4.

Ends with both players regrouping at a sealed inner door that needs a code found in two separate offices — first real code-sharing puzzle, per the original design doc.

### Act 2 — Research Labs

Introduces the glyph system and first simultaneous-action puzzle, as originally planned.

**Story beats:**
- Audio logs reveal the "signature" isn't geological — early tests showed it responding to *stimuli*, not just measurements. One scientist, Dr. Osei Farrow, becomes obsessed; her logs get increasingly warm and personal, like she's talking *to* something, not about it.
- A terminal log shows internal dissent — a junior researcher, Callum Bray, wanted to report the anomaly's behavior up the chain. He's the last log entry of his in this wing. He is never seen or mentioned again after this point in any later wing.

**Twist seed #2:** the glyph symbols players learn to sketch to each other aren't a crew-invented code. A log reveals the crew found them *already carved* into the ice core samples, predating the station by an unknown span of time. The crew adopted the symbols as their working shorthand without ever really reckoning with what that meant.

### Act 3 — Reactor & Power Wing

Cross-wing environmental sync puzzles, as originally planned — one player's actions changing the other's accessible space.

**Story beats:**
- Power logs show a deliberate, repeated pattern: every time the crew tried to cut power to the excavation site below, station systems would "spontaneously" reroute it back, logged as "unexplained feedback loops." The crew stopped trying to cut power after the third attempt. No log explains why they stopped trying — just that they did.
- **This wing introduces the first combat encounter** (see Combat section below): a maintenance drone, originally built for hull repair, has been quietly repurposed/corrupted and now patrols reactor corridors, treating anything moving as damage to be "corrected." First taste of the game *not* being purely no-conflict — framed as physical danger from station systems, not yet from something intentional.

**Twist:** a black-box flight recorder–style log (heavily corrupted, only partially recoverable — players get fragments, not the full thing) shows a coordinated, precise power surge the night contact was lost, originating from *inside* the crew's own command deck — not the excavation site. Someone on the inside pulled a plug or flipped a switch that night, deliberately.

### Act 4 — Deep Excavation Site

Radio interference is central, per the original doc — this is where your Day 3 signal-degradation mechanic finally gets to shine narratively, not just mechanically. Story reveals accelerate hard here.

**Major reveal:** the twelfth crew member — the one with no quarters, no personal effects — was never a person. "They" are a **construct the anomaly built partway through year six**, modeled from composite behavioral data of the other eleven, meant to be indistinguishable and to walk among the crew undetected. Early logs the players already read start re-reading differently in hindsight (a log that seemed like an offhand joke about "does anyone actually remember hiring the new guy" turns out to be exactly that — nobody could quite remember).

**Combat escalates:** more drones, now visibly modified with excavation-site materials fused into their chassis — the "corruption" is really the anomaly learning to *use* the station's own maintenance systems as a body. These are the first enemies explicitly tied to the anomaly itself, not just malfunctioning hardware.

**The big mid-game twist:** the black-box fragments from Act 3, once fully reconstructed here (a proper end-of-wing puzzle requiring both players' half-fragments combined), reveal Dr. Farrow herself pulled that switch — not sabotage, but a **mercy kill attempt**. She'd realized the construct was close to fully "waking up" and tried to sever power to the excavation site to stop it, permanently, even though she knew it would likely strand or kill the crew in the process. It didn't work. The crew survived the blackout. The construct didn't stop.

### Act 5 — The Core (Finale, Expanded)

Originally a small, intimate reunion space for the ending choice. Expanded here into a full climactic wing, roughly the length of Act 3, since the story now needs room for the twist payoff and the new combat/boss content.

**Structure:**
1. **The Descent** — players physically reunite in person for the first time since Act 1's separation (as in the original doc), now walking together through crystal-overgrown corridors that are unmistakably no longer just "ice."
2. **The Construct's Reveal** — players finally encounter the twelfth "crew member" directly — not a monster, not obviously hostile at first. It speaks using fragments of all eleven real crew members' recorded voices, stitched together mid-sentence, genuinely uncertain whether it's "one of them" or something else entirely. This is the emotional gut-punch beat — it isn't evil, it's *confused*, built to belong somewhere and never told what it actually is.
3. **The Choice, Recontextualized** — the original three endings (awaken / seal / communicate) return, but now carry the weight of everything players learned: Farrow's mercy-kill attempt, the eleven real crew members' fates (revealed here: not killed, but *absorbed*, non-violently, over years, into what's now speaking to you), and the construct's own uncertain plea.

**Boss-style final combat (new):** rather than a traditional health-bar boss fight (which would clash badly with the "no traditional combat feel" tone), the finale climax is a **co-op puzzle-under-pressure** sequence — the drones from Acts 3–4 converge on the Core as the construct's control over the station destabilizes mid-conversation, forcing both players to fight off/evade waves of drones *while* one of them keeps interacting with the dialogue-puzzle that determines the ending. High tension without turning the emotional climax into a generic shooter beat.

### Epilogue Wing — Aftersignal (New)

A short, quiet fifth "wing" (10–15 minutes) that plays out differently based on which ending was chosen in Act 5 — showing the aftermath: a rescue ship arriving, or not; a transmission sent back to the company, true or falsified; a final shared moment between the two players' characters, wordless, depending on tone. This is the actual title-drop moment — "aftersignal" being whatever gets sent (or doesn't) once everything is decided.

## New Characters (Named Crew)

For log/audio variety — you don't need to voice-act all of these, text logs are fine per your existing "no forced dialogue" structure.

- **Dr. Osei Farrow** — lead researcher, obsessive, ultimately sympathetic; the emotional throughline of the mystery
- **Callum Bray** — junior researcher, whistleblower instinct, vanishes early (Act 2) — his fate is deliberately never fully explained, a loose thread players can theorize about (was he the first one "absorbed," did he actually escape and just stop logging?)
- **The Construct ("Twelve," unofficially)** — the true final "character" of the game, built from the other eleven

You can keep the rest of the twelve-person roster as background flavor (names/photos in logs) without needing individual arcs for all of them — Farrow, Bray, and Twelve are the three that carry the actual plot.

## Combat System (New)

Per your request to add real combat — here's a version scoped to stay consistent with the game's tone (quiet, mysterious, no traditional shooter feel) rather than bolting on a generic system that clashes with it.

### Design Philosophy
- **Utility-based, not power-fantasy.** Players are salvagers, not soldiers — combat tools are repurposed station equipment (welding torches, signal disruptors, salvaged stun batons), not military-grade weapons.
- **Co-op relevant, not co-op redundant.** Ideally, combat encounters should have an asymmetric element too, matching your core communication philosophy — e.g. one player can see a drone's weak point via a salvaged scanner tool, the other has the disruptor that exploits it, forcing the same "share information to act" loop that already defines your puzzles.
- **Rare, escalating, and earned.** Combat shouldn't appear until Act 3 (Reactor Wing) at the earliest, so the first third of the game stays pure exploration/puzzle, true to the original tone, and combat's introduction itself feels like a tonal shift signaling "something's wrong here."

### Enemy Types
1. **Maintenance Drones** (Act 3) — slow, predictable patrol patterns, easily avoided or disabled with a well-timed EMP-style tool; introduces combat gently
2. **Fused Drones** (Act 4) — faster, more erratic, visibly crystal-corrupted; require the asymmetric scanner+disruptor combo to reliably take down
3. **Echo Fragments** (Act 5 only) — non-lethal, disorienting entities that briefly scramble a player's controls/radio (mechanically reusing your existing signal-interference system as an attack, rather than inventing a new mechanic from scratch) — these harass rather than kill, keeping the finale's tension high without needing a full health/damage system

### Player Tools (not "weapons" — keep the salvager framing)
- **Welding Torch** — short-range, can disable a drone if you get close, risky since it requires proximity
- **Signal Disruptor** — ranged, temporarily stuns Fused Drones, limited charges (encourages avoidance over spray-and-pray)
- **Scanner Attachment** — doesn't damage anything; reveals weak points/patrol routes to your partner over radio — this is the one that ties combat back into your core "share what only you can see" design pillar

### Fail State
Getting "defeated" by a drone doesn't kill the player outright (keeps with the doc's original "no harsh fail states" philosophy) — it knocks them out briefly, drops them at the nearest safe point, and the drone resets its patrol. Real stakes come from wasted time and revealed position, not instant death/reload screens.

## Multiplayer-Specific Story Beats

A few beats designed specifically to use the fact there are two players, not just "story that happens to be co-op":

- **Divergent log discovery** stays central per the original doc — but now specific *plot-critical* reveals (the black-box fragments, the roster/quarters mismatch) are hard-split so that piecing together the real timeline of events is *impossible* solo, even on a replay — reinforcing that the story itself only exists in the space between two players comparing notes.
- **The Act 5 dialogue-vs-combat split** — one player defending the Core, one actually conversing with the construct — means each player experiences the emotional climax and the mechanical climax from different vantage points, and only by talking to each other afterward do they get the full picture of what happened. Encourages a genuine "wait, what did YOU see" conversation after finishing, which is a great note to end a hackathon submission on.

## Scope Note (Practical, Not Creative)

None of the above needs building all at once. Suggested build order given everything you've already got working:
1. Finish Wing 1 (Act 1) with the roster/quarters twist seed planted — smallest lift, reuses your existing systems entirely
2. Build the drone AI + one combat tool (start with just the Welding Torch) before writing any more story wings — prove combat feels good before committing to three enemy types
3. Layer story content (audio logs, terminal text) into wings as you build them, rather than writing all twelve crew members' full logs upfront

This story can absolutely support a full day-plus playthrough once built — the risk isn't the story being too small, it's combat + expanded Act 5 being a lot of solo engineering on top of everything else. Worth revisiting scope honestly once Wing 1 + basic combat are both proven out, same way you already proved out networking before committing further.
